# HyCAN-DB v0.1 — analysis summary

A plain-language summary of what the corpus shows, as of the v0.1 analysis
(2026-10-06). Every number here is produced by code in this repository
(`src/hycan/meta.py`, `scripts/generate_figures.py`) from
`data/raw/measurements_v0.1.csv` and is reproducible from a clean clone by
running `notebooks/03_descriptive_analysis.ipynb` and
`notebooks/04_meta_analysis.ipynb`. Figures are in `figures/` with standalone
captions in `figures/CAPTIONS.md`.

## The corpus

521 measurement rows from 51 papers, spanning 1999–2025 and 13 carbon material
classes. After the §12.3 analysis filter (isothermal, exact-valued, both
conditions stated, uptake present) **431 rows across 45 papers** are eligible for
aggregation; 419 carry a wt% uptake. The dataset deliberately retains the other
90 rows — non-isothermal cycles, bounded values, unstated-condition and
characterization-only rows — because an honest corpus records them, but they are
filtered out before any mean, fit, or model (that filter is one shared function,
`hycan.load.analysis_subset`, so every figure and the model agree).

Reproducibility tiers across the full corpus: 92 A, 358 B, 54 C, 17 D. The
material mass is in activated carbons, followed by composites, doped carbons, and
the nanotube and graphene families.

## Headline finding: the Chahine rule overstates real uptake by ~30%

The Chahine rule holds that physisorptive H₂ uptake on porous carbons scales as
**1 wt% per 500 m²/g of BET area at 77 K**. Tested across the corpus with a
hierarchical (mixed-effects) through-origin regression of uptake on BET area,
with a **random intercept per paper** so that correlated measurements within a
paper do not dominate:

> **The corpus reaches ≈0.72 wt% per 500 m²/g (95% CI 0.62–0.81)** — equivalently
> **1 wt% per ~700 m²/g** — and the confidence interval excludes the rule's value
> of 1.0.

Pooled across the literature with paper-level weighting, the canonical rule
**systematically overstates** the uptake carbons actually achieve at 77 K, by
roughly 30%. This is a corpus-level statement no single paper can make.

Three supporting results:

- **It is not a modelling artifact.** A naive through-origin OLS gives a steeper
  0.86 per 500 m²/g; the gap to the hierarchical 0.72 is exactly the inflation
  the random-effects model removes — a few high-area papers pull the naive fit up.
- **It is robust to data quality.** Restricting to Tier A–B (0.70) or Tier A
  alone (0.64) does not push the slope toward 1.0; the highest-quality subset is,
  if anything, slightly lower. The shortfall is a property of the field, not of
  the inclusion threshold.
- **The relationship carries a baseline offset.** A free-intercept fit finds a
  positive intercept (~1.1 wt%) and a shallower marginal slope, so strict
  proportionality through the origin is itself an approximation.

## Publication-bias check

A paper-level funnel (each paper's mean deviation from the Chahine prediction
against its precision) with an Egger-style intercept test shows a slightly
negative mean deviation and nonzero asymmetry (Egger intercept p ≈ 0.006),
consistent with the hierarchical shortfall and with the §7.4 search's deliberate
weighting toward the 1998–2005 controversy literature. **Limitation, stated
rather than hidden:** the source papers report no per-value uncertainty, so
precision here is a within-paper dispersion proxy, not a true standard error;
read this as corroborating, not as a formal small-study test.

## Predictive model

A baseline model predicts 77 K uptake from structure and conditions on the
modeling subset (§12.3-filtered, Tier A/B, 77 K, method-confirmed BET,
`total` excluded): **191 rows across 28 papers** — nearly triple the 102 rows /
11 papers the manual's §14.1 recorded, thanks to Phase D and the verification
pass. Under **cross-validation grouped by paper** (so the model is scored only on
papers it never trained on), tree models generalize well:

| model | MAE (wt%) | R² |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LinearRegression | 0.88 ± 0.30 | 0.42 ± 0.50 |
| RandomForest | 0.47 ± 0.06 | 0.85 ± 0.07 |
| XGBoost | 0.49 ± 0.06 | 0.84 ± 0.08 |

The tree models reach **R² ≈ 0.84 (MAE ≈ 0.5 wt%)** on held-out papers; the linear
baseline is far worse because the pore-volume features are sparse. SHAP
attribution on XGBoost ranks **pressure and BET area as the dominant predictors**,
with pore volumes a clear second tier and material class contributing little — the
77 K physisorption picture. Figures 6–8 show predicted-vs-measured, the SHAP
ranking, and the uptake-vs-BET Pareto frontier; out-of-fold predictions are in
`data/processed/predictions_v0.1.csv`.

**Honest limitations:** 28 papers is adequate but not large; doping is excluded as
a feature for want of a comparable subset (§14.3); and no lab samples are overlaid
on Fig 6, because the Perez-lab samples are Raman/XPS-characterized and carry no
H₂ uptake measurement. The model and its attribution are reproducible from
`notebooks/05_ml_baseline.ipynb` and the tested `hycan.features` / `hycan.ml`.
