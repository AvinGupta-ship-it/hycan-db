- Several curves of the same colour overlap in Fig 4 (two red, two blue), Fig 3S (two red, two blue), Fig 5A and Fig 7S (all black), and `check` interpolates only one single-valued series per archive. Each curve therefore has its own archive, made by extracting all markers of the colour from the page image (`--mode markers`, colour tolerance 100 for black/red/3S colours, 80 for Fig 4 blue #3248a4), assigning each marker to its curve by pressure ordering and continuity, then whitening the same-colour pixels nearer to the other curve(s) in a copy of the page image (copies in the work directory; the archives' `source_image.path` points to them) and extracting again. For Fig 5B `--open-px 2` was needed to keep the small down-triangle at 0 bar. Marker pixel counts, positions and values in the archives are those of the tool; the whitening only removes the other curve's pixels.
- `check` against printed values (all PASS at 5% unless noted): Fig 4 a at 19.964 bar 3.892 vs 3.9 (0.20%); Fig 4 b 5.466 vs 5.5 (0.62%); Fig 4 c 6.001 vs 6.0 (0.02%); Fig 4 d at 19.94 bar 6.909 vs 6.9 (0.12%); Fig 5A b at 1.014 bar 2.565 vs 2.6 (1.36%); Fig 5B H2 and Fig 5A a at 1.002 bar 2.199 vs 2.3 (4.38%); SI Fig 5S G212 at 20 bar 3.963 vs 3.96 (0.08%); SI Fig 3S a at 19.96 bar 3.300 vs 3.3 and Fig 3S d at 19.94 bar 5.838 vs 6 (2.7%, 3% tolerance), both against the text's rounded range "between 3.3 and 6 wt %" with the sample inferred as the lowest and highest curve. No printed value exists for Fig 3S b and c or for Fig 7S, so their archives remain `unchecked`.
- Branch: the paper never prints which marker glyph is adsorption or desorption (the Fig 7S caption glyphs are blank in the PDF) and says only that both branches exist and show no hysteresis. Branch is inferred from glyph geometry: up-triangle = adsorption, down-triangle = desorption (supported by Fig 5B: an up-triangle sits at the origin and a down-triangle at 0 bar with 0.32 wt% residual uptake, the end of a desorption branch). For p >= 2.2 bar the glyphs alternate cleanly, up-triangles at integer pressures and down-triangles at half-integer pressures, and branch is taken from that pattern (the shape classifier agrees wherever it is decisive; at the small Fig 7S markers it is unreliable, so only the pattern is used). Below 2.2 bar branch comes from the glyph shape classifier; where the shape is ambiguous or overlapped the branch defaults to adsorption and the row says so. Desorption rows are therefore inferred, not printed.
- Stars: where an up- and a down-triangle coincide at one pressure (all G212 markers, the 20-bar end of every Fig 4 and Fig 3S curve, most Fig 5B markers) one row is recorded (branch adsorption) and the star counts as 2 in `isotherm_point_count`.
