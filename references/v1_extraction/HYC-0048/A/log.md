## Findings

1. Unit-consistency failure. Figs 8 and 9 plot one run. Every Fig 9a marker pairs with a Fig 8a marker at x(9a) = p/3.5 MPa, and wt%/(cm3(STP)/g) = 0.02968 to 0.02985 over 40 pairs (median 0.02977), 3.31 times the W1 factor 0.0089938. The Table 7 values are the Fig 9a values: 209.359 cm3(STP)/g at 34.791 bar (AC-HCT) is 1.883 wt% as W1, against the printed 6.12 wt%. The paper defines neither wt% convention and prints no cm3(STP)/g value. Coding: rows with both a Fig 8 and a Fig 9 marker carry the Fig 9 wt% (convention `unknown`, source `none`, as reported) and the Fig 8 value in `uptake_ml_stp_g`, with the failure stated in `notes`; rows without a Fig 9 partner carry W1 computed from cm3(STP)/g (`W1`, `computed_from_other_unit`); Table 7 merged rows carry the printed wt% and the digitized cm3 value in `notes` only. The §3 rule (W1 from the non-wt% value is used) is applied by the downstream code on the 39 rows that have both fields. The validator reports the same 39 rows.
2. The 273 K curves are pointwise rescalings of the 77 K curves: Fig 9b/Fig 9a = 0.1000 over 39 matched markers (median 0.09995, range 0.0994 to 0.1008) and Fig 8b/Fig 8a = 0.0887 to 0.0889 over 17 matched AC-HCB markers (0.0877 to 0.0929 over 11 matched AC-HCT and AC-HCS markers, whose markers overlap). Stated neutrally in `notes` of M14, M29, M48. The paper's text (journal p.142) says only a negligible amount is adsorbed at about 273 K.
