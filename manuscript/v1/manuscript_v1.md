## Abstract

The Chahine rule, that carbons adsorb roughly 1 wt% of hydrogen per
500 m² g⁻¹ of BET surface area at 77 K, is the central scaling claim of
carbon-based hydrogen storage, yet it has never been tested on a probability
sample of the literature with a pre-registered analysis. We registered a
protocol (Zenodo, 10.5281/zenodo.23719010, 2026-10-28) specifying the search,
a stratified probability sample of the corpus, independent dual extraction
with adjudication, a blinded author audit, and five research questions, then
built HyCAN-DB v1.0: 17,061 measurement rows from 124 papers (1987–2026),
80 of them a design-based sample of a 5,251-record frame. At the excess-uptake
maximum, the rule is supported on average: the pooled ratio of n_ex,max to
A_BET/500 is 0.98 (95% CI 0.91–1.05; n = 82 samples, 29 papers, 26
laboratories), within the registered ±15% margin. Restricted to the 57 samples
whose excess basis is explicitly stated rather than inferred, the interval
(0.87–1.08) crosses the margin and the registered decision is inconclusive, so
the conclusion depends on that choice. Around the compliant average, the rule
is a poor predictor: the 95% prediction interval for a single new material is
0.61–1.56, uptake rises sublinearly with area (ln–ln slope 0.75, 95% CI
0.66–0.85), and at 1 bar the ratio falls to 0.58 (0.52–0.65). Between
laboratories measuring the same commercial carbon, 1-bar uptake varies with a
pooled CV near 9%. Reporting practice is weak by design-weighted estimates:
6% of eligible papers state the uptake basis for every value, 7% state their
wt% convention, and 9% report instrument and calibration. Under paper-grouped
cross-validation, a four-term linear model predicts uptake as well as
gradient-boosted trees (out-of-fold R² 0.71 vs 0.65). The widely cited rule
survives as a corpus-level average at high pressure, and fails as a
sample-level design tool; the database, code and audit trail are released for
reuse.

## 1. Introduction

Physisorptive hydrogen storage on porous carbons has been organized for two
decades around a single empirical regularity. Chahine and Bose observed that
maximum excess uptake at 77 K scales with specific surface area at about
1 wt% per 500 m² g⁻¹ [1,2], and the proportionality, since known as the
Chahine rule, has been reproduced in influential single-laboratory studies
[3–5] and used routinely to benchmark new materials, to sanity-check
reported values, and to set targets for synthesis. The rule's evidentiary
base, however, is a convenience corpus: compilations assembled from the
papers their authors knew, dominated by specialist groups, with no defined
sampling frame, no control of duplicate re-reports, and no pre-specified
analysis. The same literature is known to carry serious reproducibility
problems: the extraordinary uptake claims of 1997–1999 on nanotubes,
nanofibers and alkali-doped carbons [6–8] failed subsequent re-examination
[9–11], a dedicated round robin found large interlaboratory disagreement on
a single distributed carbon [12], and irreproducibility remains a named,
unresolved concern of the field's own methodological literature [13,14].

Whether the rule holds is therefore two questions. Does the average across
the literature, sampled so that every eligible paper has a known inclusion
probability, sit near 1 wt% per 500 m² g⁻¹? And is the regularity tight
enough to predict what a given material will do? A corpus assembled and
analysed after seeing the data cannot answer either credibly, because
extraction, inclusion and modelling choices all move the estimate, and in a
literature this heterogeneous they move it by more than the effect of
interest. The present study was designed to remove those degrees of freedom:
the protocol, registered publicly before any search request was sent
(§ Methods), fixed the search, the two-stratum sampling design, the
extraction pipeline, the audit, the five research questions (RQ1–RQ5), every
analysis including its equivalence margin, and the sensitivity analyses,
with all deviations disclosed (§ 7).

A prior, unregistered version of this database (v0.1; 51 papers assembled by
citation chaining) produced a headline estimate that the rule overstates
uptake by about 30% (0.72 wt% per 500 m² g⁻¹). That estimate was withdrawn
by the author before any public use, for reasons the registered study now
makes quantitative: it depended on the model specification, not only on the
data (§ 6.2). The v1.0 study replaces it.

## 2. Methods

Full operational detail is in the registered protocol
(`docs/v1_protocol.md`, cited by section below); this section summarizes it.
The registration record, with file hashes, is `docs/v1_registration.md`
(Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.23719010, published 2026-10-28).

### 2.1 Search, screening and sampling frame (protocol §§4–7)

Records were harvested from OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar with registered
queries and merged on identifiers and fuzzy metadata (Table 1). Two
independent language-model agents screened every merged record at
title/abstract against the §4 criteria (primary gas-phase H₂ uptake
measurements on carbon-based sorbents), with disagreements resolved by a
registered tiebreak; the author audited a random sheet of screening decisions
before the frame was frozen. The 5,251 surviving records form the sampling
frame, stratified into S1 (records whose title/abstract evidence a 77 K
measurement reaching at least 20 bar or an explicit excess maximum; N = 176)
and S2 (all others; N = 5,075).

Papers were drawn by a registered random walk within each stratum until the
stratum's eligibility quota was reached: 106 S1 and 127 S2 records were
taken, full texts were sought for all of them (32 and 47 could not be
retrieved), 74 and 80 were assessed against §4 on full text, and 60 and 20
were eligible, giving the 80-paper probability sample. Each paper's design
weight is N_h/d_h (S1 176/106, S2 5075/127). The 51 papers of the v0.1
corpus entered separately as a PRISMA "other methods" arm: 7 were drawn by
the walk itself and belong to the probability sample, 36 were eligible and
extracted in a registered legacy mode, 7 were ineligible, and one
(HYC-0041) could not be retrieved by either permitted route and carries only
its uncoded v0.1 rows, which no §11 analysis uses (Amendment 2).

### 2.2 Extraction, reconciliation and agreement (protocol §§8–9)

Every probability-sample paper was extracted twice, by two independent
language-model agents (A and B) running frozen prompts on the same PDF with
no access to each other's output, then reconciled by a third agent (C) that
resolved every disagreement against the source with a recorded decision and
location; a separate labelling agent (L) mapped digitized figure curves to
samples. The prompts and models were frozen before the main run (Amendment
1); every v0.1 paper was re-extracted under the same frozen prompts, so no
pre-registration output enters the dataset. Agreement between the two
independent extractions is reported in full in
`references/v1_extraction/agreement_dual.json` (Table 3): across 80 papers
and 9,282 matched row pairs, row-detection agreement was 0.83, uptake values
agreed within the registered tolerance in 88.7% of pairs, BET areas in
99.9%, and the stated uptake basis in 97.0% (κ = 0.94); the inferred basis,
the field's known weak point, agreed in 80.2% (κ = 0.56), which is why the
primary analyses are repeated on stated-basis rows (sensitivity (i)).

### 2.3 Dataset and computed coding (protocol §10)

The released dataset (`data/raw/measurements_v1.0.csv`, with computed fields
in `data/processed/measurements_v1.0_computed.csv`) holds 17,061 rows from
124 papers: one row per reported measurement, validated against a versioned
schema with controlled vocabularies, explicit provenance (source location,
extraction method, reconciliation outcome) and per-row reporting items
(Table 2). Deterministic committed code adds the §10 computed fields,
including the reporting score and tier (A 1,723 / B 9,246 / C 5,218 / D 861
rows), the W1 weight-percent convention harmonization, isotherm-level
quantities (n_ex,max and its pressure and status), and a physical
plausibility screen that flagged 372 rows.

### 2.4 Author audit (protocol §12–13; deviation D1)

Before any analysis result was final, the author audited 60 rows drawn by a
registered stratified design: 30 rows at p(n_ex,max) from the isotherms that
feed RQ1 (stratum a), 15 other dual-paper rows (b) and 15 legacy-paper rows
(c), each audited field by field against the PDF without AI assistance. The
registered format was blind re-entry; before entering any value the author
chose a verification format instead (the page shows the extracted value and
he marks it against the PDF), a deviation recorded as D1 with its cost
stated: a verification audit bounds the rate of errors detectable when told
what to look for, which is weaker than blind re-extraction. The outcome was
0 errors in all 60 rows (708 field verdicts); the exact 95% upper bound on
the stratum-(a) row error rate is 11.6% (Table 4), and no registered
follow-up rule fired.

### 2.5 Analyses (protocol §11)

All analyses were specified before any extraction output was seen. RQ1
(primary): per sample, the geometric mean of eligible 77 K excess maxima
(nmax status observed-max or plateau, every point measured, no basis-shape
conflict, §11-eligible BET area ≥ 300 m² g⁻¹) enters
y = ln[n_ex,max/(A_BET/500)], modelled with random intercepts for laboratory
and paper within laboratory (REML); exp(μ̂) is the pooled ratio, its CI the
wider of a t-interval on L−1 degrees of freedom and a laboratory-cluster
bootstrap (2,000 resamples), and the registered decision compares that CI
with the equivalence margin [1/1.15, 1.15]. Thirteen registered sensitivity
analyses (i)–(xiii) and eight exploratory moderators follow §11, including
the §7 design-weighted estimate (xii) and the v0.1 model specification
applied to the new sample (xiii). RQ2 fits ln n on ln A_BET and ln P in the
0.9–1.2 bar window, and the same with N₂- or CO₂-derived micropore volume,
never mixing probes. RQ3 estimates between-laboratory CVs for materials with
identical supplier, product name and grade measured by two or more
laboratories; free-text identities were grouped by a committed map that
merges only identical printed product designations
(`references/v1_analysis/material_identity_map.csv`). RQ4 estimates
reporting-practice shares with the stratified ratio estimator [17] under the
§7 design weights and Korn–Graubard intervals [18]. RQ5 compares a physical
baseline (n = kA·P/(P+p₀)), a four-term linear model and gradient-boosted
trees [20] under GroupKFold cross-validation grouped by paper (20
partitions), scored on pooled out-of-fold predictions. Mixed models use
statsmodels [19]; BET areas enter under the §11.0 rule, which follows the
IUPAC guidance on reporting physisorption areas [16]. Software is pinned in
`requirements-v1.lock`; every analysis is a committed script and every
count-bearing table is generated by committed code (§14).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Study flow and dataset

Figure 1 (fig_v1_prisma) and Table 1 give the PRISMA 2020 flow [15]: 11,238
merged records, 5,987 excluded at double title/abstract screening, a frame
of 5,251, 233 records taken by the sampling walk, 79 not retrievable, 154
assessed and 80 eligible. The dataset holds 17,061 rows, 878 samples and 177
measuring laboratories across 124 papers spanning 1987–2026 (Table 2).
Retrieval failure is the largest loss in the flow (30% of S1 and 37% of S2
draws), almost entirely paywalled or vanished full texts; the §7 design
treats these as taken records, so the weights do not correct for them, and
results generalize to the retrievable literature.

### 3.2 Verification and audit

Independent dual extraction agreed at the levels in Table 3; the
reconciliation stage resolved every disagreement with a recorded decision.
The author audit found 0 errors in 60 rows (Table 4). Because the audit
followed the D1 verification format, we report it as bounding the
detectable row error rate in the RQ1-contributing cells at 11.6% (exact
95%), not as the registered blind re-extraction.

### 3.3 RQ1: the rule at the excess maximum

From 545 coded 77 K excess isotherms, the registered ladder keeps 108 with
an observed maximum or plateau, 107 with every usable point measured, and 94
(29 papers) with an eligible area of at least 300 m² g⁻¹, collapsing to 82
sample-level units from 26 laboratories (Table 5; Figure 2,
fig_v1_rq1_ratio). The pooled geometric-mean ratio of n_ex,max to A_BET/500
is **0.976**, with 95% CI 0.905–1.052 (t) and 0.909–1.047 (bootstrap). Both
intervals lie inside the registered margin [0.870, 1.150], so the registered
decision is **supported**: on average, the retrievable literature's maxima
sit within 15% of the Chahine value. The pressure at the maximum has
quartiles 20–40 bar, with 20 of 94 isotherms peaking outside 10–60 bar.

The average conceals most of the practical content. The variance components
put essentially all dispersion between samples within paper (0.031 on the
ln scale) and between laboratories (0.020), and the 95% prediction interval
for a new sample from a new laboratory is **0.61–1.56**: a material matching
the rule's average can still miss its own prediction by ±50%. The secondary
ln–ln slope is 0.754 (0.659–0.850), rising only to 0.896 under a generous
20% area-error correction: uptake grows sublinearly with area, so high-area
materials fall increasingly short of the rule, which is also why the
all-areas sensitivity below moves the ratio up.

**Sensitivities (Table 6; Figure 3, fig_v1_rq1_sensitivity).** Nine of the
eleven decision-bearing variants reproduce "supported", including dropping flagged
rows (iv), tiers A/B only (viii), excluding composites and metal-loaded
samples (ix), treating unknown wt% conventions as W2 (vii), the design-
weighted estimate (xii, 0.924 [0.893, 1.001]) and all §4-eligible papers
(iii). Leave-one-laboratory-out moves the ratio only within 0.963–0.994
(xi). Two variants do not: restricting to **stated** excess values (i),
n = 57, gives 0.967 with CI 0.865–1.081, crossing the lower margin by 0.005,
decision **inconclusive**; per the registered robustness rule this
qualification is carried in the abstract and conclusions. And admitting all
positive areas (vi) gives 1.151 [0.864, 1.598], inconclusive, driven by
low-area carbons that overperform per unit area, consistent with the
sublinear slope. Variants (v) and (x) equal the primary exactly: no primary
sample has an area in [300, 500) m² g⁻¹, and no fitted-interpolated value
survives the other filters. No exploratory moderator's CI excludes zero
(Table 7).

Under the v0.1 model specification applied to the new probability sample
(xiii), through-origin pooling of all 77 K rows at every pressure, the
estimate is 0.576 wt% per 500 m² g⁻¹ [0.553, 0.600], far outside the margin
(§ 6.2).

### 3.4 RQ2: the rule at 1 bar

In the 0.9–1.2 bar window (188 samples, 50 papers), uptake reaches only
**0.581** of A_BET/500 (0.519–0.650), and the area exponent is 0.573
(0.501–0.645) (Table 8; Figure 4, fig_v1_rq2_one_bar): at low pressure the
rule fails in level and in slope, as micropore filling rather than surface
coverage controls uptake. BET area nevertheless beats N₂-derived micropore
volume as a predictor on the same 108 samples (ML AIC −21.4 vs 29.4), the
opposite of the common assumption that V_micro is the better low-pressure
descriptor; the CO₂-derived comparison is underpowered (8 samples). Results
on all §4-eligible papers are the same within intervals.

### 3.5 RQ3: the same material across laboratories

Six commercial carbons were measured by two or more laboratories under
identical product designations (Table 9; Figure 5, fig_v1_rq3_materials).
The pooled between-laboratory CV of 1-bar uptake is **8.6%** (5 df, 95% CI
5.4–21.1%); BET area 8.2% (2 df), n_ex,max 2.8% (1 df). These are thin
estimates, limited mainly by two registered row filters that exclude the
2009 round robin's interpolated per-laboratory values, and by one
identical-value pair (two papers from one university reporting the same
numbers as their own work) that is retained as coded and disclosed
(`references/v1_analysis/README.md`). Taken at face value, an
interlaboratory CV near 9% in the best case means single-laboratory
differences below roughly 20% between materials should not be interpreted
as material effects.

### 3.6 RQ4: reporting practice

Design-weighted shares of eligible papers meeting basic reporting items are
low (Table 10; Figure 6, fig_v1_rq4_items): 6.1% state the uptake basis for
every value (a), 7.1% state their wt% convention (b), 8.5% report instrument
and calibration (d), 0.9% report the free-volume calibration gas and
temperature (j); only degassing conditions exceed one half (52.6%). The
weighted shares sit well below the unweighted counts (for example H₂ purity:
40/80 papers, weighted 24.4%) because the small specialist stratum S1
reports far better than the large general stratum S2 that dominates the
weights; the literature a reader samples at random is worse than the
specialist literature suggests. Period contrasts show improvement after 2015
on uncertainty, sample mass, degassing and basis (1% to 13%, still low), and
a decline on the wt% convention (9% to 4%).

### 3.7 RQ5: how predictable is uptake?

Under paper-grouped cross-validation on 6,404 rows from 57 papers, the
four-term linear model (A_BET, ln P, interaction) attains pooled out-of-fold
R² **0.714** (2.5–97.5 percentile across partitions 0.698–0.731), the
physical Langmuir-form baseline 0.637, and gradient-boosted trees with the
registered features 0.647 (Table 11; Figure 7, fig_v1_rq5_models). Grouping
by laboratory and weighting rows inversely to paper size preserve the
ordering. All models degrade sharply in the ≥10 bar regime (R² 0.13–0.33),
where the maxima live: the features the literature reports do not determine
high-pressure uptake, which is the prediction-interval finding of RQ1 seen
from the machine-learning side. With leakage-proof validation there is no
evidence that flexible models extract more from these descriptors than a
linear fit.

## 4. Strengths

The design removes the main degrees of freedom that have made this
literature's syntheses unreliable: a registered protocol with a public
timestamp and file hashes before the first search request; a probability
sample with known weights rather than a convenience corpus; two independent
extractions of every sampled paper with adjudication and released agreement
statistics; a pre-committed audit with a scored outcome; analyses, margins
and sensitivity sets fixed in advance, with the two inconclusive variants
reported as prominently as the ten supportive ones; and a release in which
every count in this manuscript regenerates from committed code.

## 5. Limitations

Retrieval failure (79 of 233 taken records) is the largest threat to
generalizability; conclusions cover the retrievable literature. The basis of
an uptake value had to be inferred for most rows (basis_inferred agreement
κ = 0.56), and the stated-basis sensitivity is inconclusive, so the primary
RQ1 statement rests partly on inference the field's own reporting forces.
The audit followed the D1 verification format rather than the registered
blind re-entry, and bounds only the detectable error rate. RQ3 is thin, with
its caveats stated in § 3.5. Extraction was performed by language-model
agents; the released agreement statistics and the clean 60-row human audit
bound, but do not eliminate, correlated extraction error. The analysis of
heterogeneous published values cannot correct errors in the underlying
measurements themselves.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 What survives of the Chahine rule

As a corpus-level average at the excess maximum, the rule survives a
registered equivalence test on a probability sample: 0.98 of the canonical
value, within ±15%, robust to tiering, flags, composites, convention
assumptions and single laboratories. Three quantified failures surround that
average. It is not a sample-level predictor (prediction interval 0.61–1.56).
It is not a low-pressure rule (0.58 of the canonical value at 1 bar, area
exponent 0.57). And it flattens at high area (ln–ln slope 0.75), so the
materials for which designers most want the rule, the highest-area carbons,
are the ones it overestimates. The practical reading for materials
development: area buys uptake at a discount that grows with area, and a
reported uptake within ±50% of the rule carries almost no information about
measurement quality.

### 6.2 Why v0.1 said 0.72

The withdrawn v0.1 estimate (0.72 wt% per 500 m² g⁻¹) is reproduced in
direction by sensitivity (xiii): the v0.1 specification, applied to the new
probability sample, gives 0.576. The gap between 0.58 and 0.98 on identical
data is the model, not the corpus: through-origin pooling of every 77 K row
at every pressure lets the dense low-pressure points, where uptake is far
below the rule (§ 3.4), dominate a nominally "maximum uptake" claim. The
registered estimand, the per-sample excess maximum with hierarchical
pooling, is the quantity the rule actually asserts. This is a concrete
demonstration that specification freedom, not extraction error, was the
dominant risk in earlier syntheses, including our own.

### 6.3 The reporting problem is the storage problem

The weighted reporting estimates (§ 3.6) quantify why synthesis of this
literature is hard: the median eligible paper does not say whether its
headline number is excess or absolute, which convention its wt% uses, or how
its instrument was calibrated. The agreement statistics show the
consequence: machines (and readers) can recover stated facts almost
perfectly (κ ≈ 0.94) but must infer the unstated ones at κ ≈ 0.56. Best
practices for exactly these items have been published for over a decade
[21]; the design-weighted estimates show they have not reached the general
literature. The single cheapest improvement available to the field is not a
new material; it is stating the basis and convention next to every uptake
value.

## 8. Data and code availability

The dataset (CC BY 4.0) and all code (MIT) are in the public repository and
will be archived with a versioned Zenodo DOI at release: measurement rows
with provenance, the data dictionary, every screening and eligibility
decision, the sampling frame and walk records, raw search-API responses, all
agent outputs and reconciliation records, the audit draw, verdicts and
score, the analysis outputs, and the registered protocol with its amendment
history. PDFs are not redistributed. Every figure and table regenerates with
`scripts/v1_search/analysis.py`, `prisma.py`, `tables.py` and `figures.py`
on a fresh clone with `requirements-v1.lock`.
