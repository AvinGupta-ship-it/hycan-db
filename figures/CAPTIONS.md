# Figure captions — HyCAN-DB Phase 5

Each caption stands alone and states the figure's takeaway and which rows it
excludes. Figures are generated by `scripts/generate_figures.py` from
`data/raw/measurements_v0.1.csv` (521 rows, 51 papers); every uptake-aggregation
figure applies the §12.3 analysis filter via `hycan.load.analysis_subset`.
Figures 6–8 are produced with the machine-learning stage.

## Figure 1 — Corpus map
Number of papers per publication year, stacked by material class; a paper is
counted once per class it reports in a year. The full 51-paper corpus (1999–2025,
13 material classes) is shown — corpus composition, not an uptake aggregation, so
the §12.3 filter is not applied here. Classes past the nine most common are
pooled as "other (rare)".
**Takeaway:** the corpus spans a quarter-century and thirteen carbon families, but
its mass is in activated carbons, with carbon nanotubes concentrated in the
classic 2001–2010 window and doped carbons and composites arriving after ~2016.

## Figure 2 — Measurement condition space
Every measurement with both temperature and pressure stated (464 of 521 rows;
35 rows have no temperature and 51 no pressure, and are necessarily absent),
plotted as temperature against pressure (log scale), colored by material class.
**Takeaway:** the field measures in two tight clusters — ~77 K cryogenic and
~298 K ambient — with almost nothing in between and a thin high-temperature tail;
the intermediate-temperature regime is essentially unexplored.

## Figure 3 — The Chahine rule across material classes (77 K)
H₂ uptake (wt%) against BET surface area for the 77 K analysis subset
(205 rows, 30 papers), with the Chahine rule (1 wt% per 500 m²/g) overlaid.
Excludes, via §12.3: non-isothermal, non-exact, and unstated-condition rows;
and, specifically here, rows whose area is not method-confirmed BET (§10.3) and
the 14 `total`-uptake rows (the rule bounds adsorbed, not total, uptake).
`unspecified`-type rows are kept (the corpus norm, §B.5).
**Takeaway:** uptake scales with BET area roughly as the Chahine rule predicts,
but the corpus-wide slope is **≈1 wt% per 580 m²/g** (0.00173 wt%·g/m²; Tier A/B
only, ≈1 per 592), about 14% below the canonical 1/500 — the rule holds in form
across material classes while overstating the level the literature actually
achieves. The formal slope with its confidence interval is in
`notebooks/04_meta_analysis.ipynb`.

## Figure 4 — Doping effect on area-normalised uptake
Area-normalised uptake (wt% per 1000 m²/g) for doped versus undoped samples,
faceted by temperature regime, over the analysis subset with a method-confirmed
BET area. At 77 K: undoped n=186 (6 points above the 8 wt%/1000 m²/g axis cap),
doped n=33 (2 off-scale); at 298 K: undoped n=38, doped n=4. Per-area
normalisation is unstable where BET is small, which is why a few points sit far
above the bulk and the axis is capped with the off-scale count annotated.
**Takeaway:** at 77 K doped and undoped carbons have essentially the same
area-normalised uptake (median ≈1.8 wt% per 1000 m²/g for both) — doping raises
total uptake chiefly by adding surface area, not by raising uptake per unit area.
Unlike the v1.0 specification (4 doped rows, one paper), this now rests on doped
samples from several papers, though the doped set remains smaller than the
undoped and heteroatom type is pooled.

## Figure 5 — Mean uptake by year and reproducibility tier
Mean H₂ uptake per publication-year bin, split by reproducibility tier, over the
wt%-bearing analysis subset. Bin row counts: 95–00 = 3, 01–05 = 70, 06–10 = 128,
11–15 = 36, 16–20 = 76, 21– = 106.
**Takeaway:** the 1995–2000 bin is entirely Tier D (the discredited early
high-uptake era), while recent bins are dominated by Tier A/B — but a single
recent Tier D claim (21– bin) still reports the highest mean of all, a reminder
that implausible values did not end with the early controversy. Two caveats: the
2006–2010 bin holds the most rows and will dominate any unweighted comparison,
and the corpus was assembled partly by deliberate search for the 1998–2005
controversy literature (§7.4), so bin sizes reflect the search protocol, not the
field's publication volume.
